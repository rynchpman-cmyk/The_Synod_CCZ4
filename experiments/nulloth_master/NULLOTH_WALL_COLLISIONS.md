# Nulloth Wall Structures and Collisions

## Structure

The activated canonical coherence potential has two vacua
(\varphi=\pm v), where

\[
  v=\sqrt{-m_{\rm eff}^2/\lambda_\varphi}.
\]

It therefore admits (Z_2) domain walls.  Their natural thickness is

\[
  \delta=\sqrt{2/(-m_{\rm eff}^2)}.
\]

For the registered carrier density (d=0.18), the vacuum amplitude is
(0.06764613810115104) and the wall thickness is
(2.9565619799454135).  A periodic domain contains a wall and an antiwall so
that the field returns to its starting vacuum.

## Controlled collision

Campaign `NUL-NLN-002` initializes the two walls with equal inward speed
(0.4).  The canonical scalar PDE—not an imposed analytic trajectory—then
advances the collision.  The walls approach to a measured minimum separation
near 1.2, interact, and re-emerge with two zero crossings in all five runs.
No annihilation occurs in this registered velocity and separation regime.

The incoming wall speed is recovered within (0.0022).  The field remains
below the declared coherence-amplitude limit, the scalar reduction constraint
remains at roundoff, and the vacuum-subtracted scalar energy is conserved to
approximately one part in (10^{10}).

## Dimensional and cascade controls

The one-dimensional collision is repeated at 64, 96, and 128 points.  Its
final high-wavenumber quadratic-energy fraction decreases as resolution is
increased, from (9.51\times10^{-7}) to (4.75\times10^{-7}).  The observed
short-wavelength content is therefore not a growing grid-scale pileup over
this ladder.

The 64-point wall profile is also embedded as a planar wall in two and three
dimensions.  Because the transverse background is exactly homogeneous, the
embedded solutions agree with the one-dimensional result to roundoff.  This
qualifies planar dimensional embedding only; corrugation, transverse breakup,
curved walls, and fully localized bubbles remain open.
