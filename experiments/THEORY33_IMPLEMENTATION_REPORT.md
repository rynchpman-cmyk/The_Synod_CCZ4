## Implemented material state

The physical currents are

\[
N^\mu=n_bu^\mu,
\qquad
C_D^\mu=n_Dv_D^\mu,
\qquad
J_D^\mu=g_DC_D^\mu.
\]

The primitive state is

\[
(n_b,\sigma,u^i,n_D,v_D^i).
\]

The implementation evolves

- densitized baryon number;
- densitized carrier source charge \(g_D\sqrt\gamma n_DW_D\);
- total material-plus-vector energy and momentum;
- thermal entropy;
- the three densitized carrier canonical momenta;
- the complete existing CCZ4 and mixed-Proca state.

`target_charge` in the shared state layout now denotes the carrier source
charge. `target_current` denotes the carrier canonical momentum in the Theory
3.3 backend. Checkpoints use explicit names so these semantics cannot be
confused with Theory 3.2.

## Master-function gate

The implementation evaluates

\[
\Lambda_{\rm M1}
=-\rho_0(n_b,n_D,\sigma)
-a_1\mathcal W
-\frac{a_2}{2M_D^4}\mathcal W^2,
\qquad
\mathcal W=x^2-n_bn_D,
\]

including the ideal-gas baryon energy, constant-sound-speed carrier energy,
chemical anchoring term, and invariant entrainment terms. The stress is

\[
T^{\mu}{}_{\nu}
=\Psi\delta^\mu{}_{\nu}
+N^\mu\mu_\nu
+C_D^\mu\chi_\nu.
\]

The executable finite metric variation holds both densitized pullback
currents fixed. For the reference state, it agrees with the analytic Carter
stress to

```text
maximum absolute error = 1.4343436161023959e-11
```

There is no interaction stress added on top of this tensor and no reservoir
energy.

## Characteristic and counterflow gate

The 1D local principal symbol is generated by differentiating the two current
conservation laws and two momentum-vorticity equations with respect to the
primitive current variables. It is not a hand-built target-speed block.

The recorded default-domain scan covers baryon densities from \(10^{-3}\) to
\(10^{-1}\), specific internal energies \(0.1\) and \(1\), and opposed
counterflows throughout \(1\le\gamma_D\le2\).

## Multifluid recovery and evolution

Recovery simultaneously solves for both proper densities and both spatial
four-velocities. Thermal entropy fixes the ideal-gas internal energy. The
solver then closes both Eulerian number densities, total material energy and
momentum, and all three carrier canonical momenta.

This is an overdetermined consistency check rather than a residual energy
assignment. A damped least-squares Newton solve is used cell by cell.  Because
independent finite-volume energy and entropy fluxes do not remain exactly on
that redundant manifold at nonlinear-gradient RK stages, production33 also
uses a square nine-variable energy inversion as a constitutive projection.
The projection changes only the represented entropy; both currents, total
energy-momentum, and carrier canonical momentum remain untouched. Complete
periodic steps are rejected if their integrated entropy decreases.

Carrier number is evolved conservatively. The spatial carrier-vorticity
equation is evolved in densitized canonical-momentum form. Its genuine
nonconservative principal product is discretized using a midpoint
straight-path interface product; it is not treated as a lower-order source.

The total material-plus-vector stress supplies energy, momentum, and CCZ4
sources. The B-field exchange remains internal to the combined conservative
system, while carrier momentum receives the locked force
\(+H_{i\lambda}J_D^\lambda\).

Finite grids use one-sided outgoing material/carrier fluxes and the retained
radiative, Gauss-compatible mixed-Proca boundary. Periodic and finite smoke
runs both pass. A campaign family of relativistic multifluid shock tubes
remains validation work; the reference path-conservative discretization is
now present for that work.

## Entropy-positive stiff drag

The differential closure is

\[
r_D^\mu=\mathcal R_D(u^\mu-\gamma_Dv_D^\mu),
\qquad
H_R=\mathcal R_D(\gamma_D^2-1)\ge0.
\]

Production evolution uses a local implicit collision solve. It exponentially
decays relative spatial four-velocity while solving simultaneously for the
post-collision baryon motion, carrier motion, proper densities, and entropy.
It preserves baryon number, carrier number, target charge, and total material
energy-momentum.

The stiff test advances ten relaxation times in one collision step. Relative
motion decays, entropy increases, and all conserved groups remain unchanged
to roundoff. The explicit Theory 3.2 target-relaxation source CFL is removed.

## Mixed-Proca damping

The Theory 3.2 exact electric matrix exponential, evolved longitudinal
charges, temporal-potential reconstruction, Gauss correction, and complete
before/after vector stress are retained.

Only the exact positive electric loss changes thermal entropy. The remaining
finite vector change must be represented by recovered multifluid chemical,
kinetic, and entrainment energy. Tests verify unchanged combined energy and
momentum, positive entropy, unchanged Gauss residuals, and successful
post-damping recovery without a reservoir.

## Operational interfaces

The backend provides periodic and finite Cartesian evolution, CCZ4 coupling,
mixed-Proca cleaning, conservative carrier and baryon fluxes, exact vector
damping, implicit carrier drag, restart-safe checkpoints, and JSON
diagnostics. The checkpoint format is
`causal_repair.production.theory33.v1`.

Carrier charge and canonical momentum use the existing Theory 3 AMR and halo
field shapes, so conservative transfer, grouped reflux, and Gauss projection
remain structurally compatible. Hardware MPI/CuPy and long adaptive campaigns
retain the qualification boundary stated for Theory 3.2.

## Qualification boundary

The derivation and reference-solver gates in the Theory 3.3 specification now
have executable implementations and passing default-domain tests. This makes
`production33` the solver path for Theory 3.3 experiments.

It remains a production-reference numerical-relativity solver, not a
campaign-qualified multi-node code. Non-default entrainment domains,
near-vacuum carrier limits, counterflows beyond \(\gamma_D=2\), long
strong-field AMR evolutions, and independent-code comparisons require new
qualification evidence rather than extrapolation from the recorded gates.
